# F3: Newer Preference Alignment Methods (SimPO, KTO, ORPO, RLOO, PRM, Self-Rewarding, RLAIF)

## Findings

### [1] SimPO uses average log-probability as a reference-free implicit reward and consistently outperforms DPO across multiple base models and benchmarks
- quote: "SimPO, a simpler yet more effective approach. The effectiveness of SimPO is attributed to a key design: using the average log probability of a sequence as the implicit reward. This reward formulation better aligns with model generation and eliminates the need for a reference model, making it more compute and memory efficient."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14734
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-05-23 (updated 2024-11-01)
- confidence: high

### [2] SimPO outperforms DPO by up to 6.4 points on AlpacaEval 2 and up to 7.5 points on Arena-Hard; a Gemma-2-9B-it model achieved 72.4% LC win rate on AlpacaEval 2
- quote: "Our results demonstrate that SimPO consistently and significantly outperforms existing approaches without substantially increasing response length. Specifically, SimPO outperforms DPO by up to 6.4 points on AlpacaEval 2 and by up to 7.5 points on Arena-Hard."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14734
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-05-23
- confidence: high

### [3] KTO matches or exceeds DPO at scales from 1B to 30B while only requiring a binary signal of whether an output is desirable, not pairwise preferences
- quote: "KTO, and it matches or exceeds the performance of preference-based methods at scales from 1B to 30B, despite only learning from a binary signal of whether an output is desirable."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01306
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-02 (ICML 2024)
- confidence: high

### [4] ORPO combines SFT and preference alignment into a single monolithic phase without a reference model and achieves strong results on Phi-2, Llama-2, and Mistral
- quote: "fine-tuning Phi-2 (2.7B), Llama-2 (7B), and Mistral (7B) with ORPO on the UltraFeedback alone surpasses the performance of state-of-the-art language models with more than 7B and 13B parameters: achieving up to 12.20% on AlpacaEval_2.0, 66.19% on IFEval, and 7.32 in MT-Bench."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07691
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-03-12
- confidence: high

### [5] Self-Play Fine-Tuning (SPIN) progressively improves an LLM by having it distinguish its own generated responses from human data, outperforming DPO with GPT-4 preference data on several benchmarks
- quote: "Our results show that SPIN can significantly improve the LLM's performance across a variety of benchmarks and even outperform models trained through direct preference optimization (DPO) supplemented with extra GPT-4 preference data."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01335
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-01-02 (ICML 2024)
- confidence: high

### [6] Self-Rewarding Language Models use LLM-as-a-Judge to provide their own rewards during Iterative DPO training; Llama 2 70B after 3 iterations outperformed Claude 2, Gemini Pro, and GPT-4 0613 on AlpacaEval 2.0
- quote: "Fine-tuning Llama 2 70B on three iterations of our approach yields a model that outperforms many existing systems on the AlpacaEval 2.0 leaderboard, including Claude 2, Gemini Pro, and GPT-4 0613."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10020
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-01-18 (ICML 2024)
- confidence: high

### [7] OpenAI's process-supervised reward model (PRM) significantly outperforms outcome supervision on math reasoning; step-level feedback on the MATH dataset achieved 78% solve rate
- quote: "we find that process supervision significantly outperforms outcome supervision for training models to solve problems from the challenging MATH dataset. Our process-supervised model solves 78% of problems from a representative subset of the MATH test set."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-31
- confidence: high

### [8] Constitutional AI (RLAIF) demonstrates that AI feedback can substitute for human labels in harmlessness training by using a list of principles and self-critique, reducing reliance on human annotation
- quote: "We experiment with methods for training a harmless AI assistant through self-improvement, without any human labels identifying harmful outputs. The only human oversight is provided through a list of rules or principles... we use 'RL from AI Feedback' (RLAIF)."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-12-15
- confidence: high

### [9] Statistical Rejection Sampling Optimization (RSO) sources preference data from the target optimal policy using rejection sampling, consistently outperforming DPO and SLiC across three diverse tasks
- quote: "Through extensive experiments across three diverse tasks, we demonstrate that RSO consistently outperforms both SLiC and DPO on evaluations from both Large Language Model (LLM) and human raters."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06657
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-09-13 (ICLR 2024)
- confidence: high

### [10] KTO's underlying theory suggests that no single human-aware loss (HALO) is universally superior; the best loss depends on the inductive biases most appropriate for a given setting
- quote: "More broadly, our work suggests that there is no one HALO that is universally superior; the best loss depends on the inductive biases most appropriate for a given setting, an oft-overlooked consideration."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01306
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-02 (ICML 2024)
- confidence: high

### [11] The LAB method (IBM) uses taxonomy-guided synthetic data generation and multi-phase tuning to scale alignment without expensive human annotations or GPT-4, offering a cost-effective alternative
- quote: "LAB-trained models can achieve competitive performance across several benchmarks compared to models trained with traditional human-annotated or GPT-4 generated synthetic data."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01081
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-03-02
- confidence: medium
