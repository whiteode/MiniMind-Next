# F4: GRPO and Reasoning-Focused Alignment Methods

## Findings

### [1] GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) was introduced by DeepSeek in the DeepSeekMath paper (Feb 2024) as a PPO variant that eliminates the critic/value network, using group-normalized advantages instead.
- quote: "Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), that enhances mathematical reasoning abilities while concurrently optimizing the memory usage of PPO."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-05
- confidence: high

### [2] DeepSeek-R1 (published in Nature, Sept 2025) uses GRPO with rule-based rewards (accuracy + format rewards) as its core RL algorithm, deliberately avoiding neural reward models for reasoning — the model achieved AIME 2024 pass@1 of 77.9% (from 15.6% baseline).
- quote: "We use GRPO as our RL algorithm... we abstain from applying neural reward models—whether outcome-based or process-based—to reasoning tasks. This decision is predicated on our observation that neural reward models are susceptible to reward hacking during large-scale RL."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-17
- confidence: high

### [3] DeepSeek-R1-Zero — the pure GRPO variant applied directly to the base model without any supervised fine-tuning (SFT) — autonomously developed emergent reasoning behaviors including self-reflection, verification, and the "aha moment" where the model spontaneously began re-evaluating its own reasoning during training.
- quote: "The proposed RL framework facilitates the emergent development of advanced reasoning patterns, such as self-reflection, verification and dynamic strategy adaptation... DeepSeek-R1-Zero exhibits an 'aha moment', characterized by a sudden increase in the use of the word 'wait' during reflections."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-01-22
- confidence: high

### [4] OpenAI's o3 and o4-mini (April 2025) use "large-scale reinforcement learning on chains of thought" but the exact RL algorithm (PPO, GRPO, or other) is not publicly disclosed; the system card only states that o-series models are "trained with large-scale reinforcement learning on chains of thought."
- quote: "The OpenAI o-series models are trained with large-scale reinforcement learning on chains of thought."
- url: https://openai.com/index/o3-o4-mini-system-card/
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-04-16
- confidence: medium (OpenAI has not disclosed the exact RL algorithm)

### [5] OpenAI's own research on process reward models (PRMs) — "Let's Verify Step by Step" (May 2023) — found that process supervision significantly outperforms outcome supervision for training models on MATH, and released the PRM800K dataset of 800K step-level human feedback labels.
- quote: "process supervision significantly outperforms outcome supervision for training models to solve problems from the challenging MATH dataset. Our process-supervised model solves 78% of problems from a representative subset of the MATH test set."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20050
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023-05-31
- confidence: high

### [6] DeepSeek-R1 explicitly chose rule-based rewards over PRMs/ORMs for reasoning tasks, arguing that neural reward models (both outcome-based and process-based) are susceptible to reward hacking during large-scale RL and require costly retraining.
- quote: "We abstain from applying neural reward models—whether outcome-based or process-based—to reasoning tasks... neural reward models are susceptible to reward hacking during large-scale RL. Moreover, retraining such models necessitates substantial computational resources and introduces further complexity into the training pipeline."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-17
- confidence: high

### [7] OpenAI's Competitive Programming paper (Feb 2025) validated that scaling general-purpose RL (o3) outperforms domain-specific hand-crafted inference strategies (o1-ioi), with o3 achieving gold at IOI 2024 without domain-specific heuristics.
- quote: "scaling general-purpose reinforcement learning, rather than relying on domain-specific techniques, offers a robust path toward state-of-the-art AI in reasoning domains, such as competitive programming."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06807
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-02-03
- confidence: high

### [8] GRPO's key innovation over PPO is removing the critic (value function) network entirely; advantages are computed by normalizing rewards within each group of sampled responses, significantly reducing memory and compute requirements.
- quote: "For each question q, GRPO samples a group of outputs {o_1, o_2, ..., o_G} from the old policy and then optimizes the policy model" with advantage computed as A_i = (r_i - mean({r_1,...,r_G})) / std({r_1,...,r_G})
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-17
- confidence: high

### [9] DeepSeek-R1 uses a multi-stage pipeline: (1) cold-start SFT data, (2) reasoning-focused GRPO with rule-based rewards, (3) rejection sampling + SFT on both reasoning and non-reasoning data, (4) a second RL stage with model-based rewards for helpfulness/harmlessness.
- quote: "DeepSeek-R1, a model trained through a multistage learning framework that integrates rejection sampling, RL and supervised fine-tuning... In the initial stage, we collect thousands of cold-start data that exhibit a conversational, human-aligned thinking process."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-17
- confidence: high

### [10] The RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards) paradigm — using GRPO with rule-based, verifiable rewards for reasoning — has become the dominant approach for open-source reasoning models following DeepSeek-R1, adopted by projects like Open R1, Qwen2.5-Math-RM, and various community implementations.
- quote: "Rule-based rewards... accuracy rewards evaluate whether the response is correct... format rewards enforce specific formatting requirements... we abstain from applying neural reward models."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-17
- confidence: medium (specific adoption numbers for community projects would need additional verification)
