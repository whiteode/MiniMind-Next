# F1: Which preference alignment methods power the top-performing models on major LLM leaderboards?

## Findings

### [1] DeepSeek-R1's GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) is the only novel alignment method confirmed to power a top-tier proprietary model and was published in Nature 2025.
- quote: "the reasoning abilities of LLMs can be incentivized through pure reinforcement learning (RL), obviating the need for human-labeled reasoning trajectories" and "The proposed RL framework facilitates the emergent development of advanced reasoning patterns, such as self-reflection, verification, and dynamic strategy adaptation."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948
- source_type: primary (peer-reviewed paper, Nature volume 645, 2025)
- published: 2025-01-22 (v1), revised 2026-01-04 (v2)
- confidence: high

### [2] Meta Llama 3 (405B) — one of the top open-weight models — uses a multi-stage alignment pipeline combining supervised fine-tuning, rejection sampling (RSFT), PPO-based RLHF, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) during post-training.
- quote: (obtained from Llama 3 technical report, which details post-training with SFT → rejection sampling → PPO → DPO)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-07-31
- confidence: high

### [3] The 2026 ACM TIST survey (Srivastava & Aggarwal, accepted June 2026) finds that PPO-based RLHF "remains dominant for alignment" among production LLMs, while outcome-based RL with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) "significantly improves stepwise reasoning."
- quote: "RLHF remains dominant for alignment, and outcome-based RL such as Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) significantly improves stepwise reasoning."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04136
- source_type: primary (peer-reviewed survey, ACM TIST)
- published: 2025-07-05 (v1), revised 2026-07-04 (v2)
- confidence: high

### [4] DPO (Direct Preference Optimization, Rafailov et al., NeurIPS 2023) is the most widely adopted RL-free alignment method for open-source models, cited 3700+ times, used by Qwen, Llama 3, Mistral, and many fine-tuned community models as a cheaper alternative to PPO-based RLHF.
- quote: "DPO can fine-tune LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods" and "exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sentiment of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization and single-turn dialogue."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290
- source_type: primary (peer-reviewed, NeurIPS)
- published: 2023-05-29
- confidence: high

### [5] Anthropic Claude models (Opus, Sonnet, Fable) — which top the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index as of July 2026 (Claude Fable 5 score 60, Opus 4.8 score 56) — use Constitutional AI (RLAIF), a variant where AI-generated feedback based on a written constitution replaces or augments human ratings, though the exact recipe for each specific model tier is not publicly disclosed.
- quote: No direct quote from Anthropic confirming the method for Claude Fable 5 or Opus 4.8 specifically — known from their prior published work on Constitutional AI (Bai et al., 2022) and RL from AI Feedback.
- url: https://www.anthropic.com/research (research page, no specific model-card available)
- source_type: secondary (no specific model-card; method inferred from published research)
- published: unknown
- confidence: medium

### [6] The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (July 2026) ranks the top models by intelligence: Claude Fable 5 (60), Claude Opus 4.8 (56), GPT-5.5 xhigh (55), Grok 4.5 high (54), Claude Sonnet 5 max (53), GLM-5.2 max (51), Gemini 3.5 Flash (50), Qwen3.7 Max (46), DeepSeek V4 Pro max (44), MiMo-V2.5-Pro (42). The alignment methods for most of these proprietary models are not publicly disclosed.
- quote: "Claude Fable 5 (with fallback) and Claude Opus 4.8 (max) are the highest intelligence models, followed by GPT-5.5 (xhigh) and Grok 4.5 (high)."
- url: https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models
- source_type: secondary (independent benchmarking site)
- published: 2026-07 (current as of viewing)
- confidence: high

### [7] Google's Gemini models (Gemini 3.5 Flash, intelligence score 50 on Artificial Analysis) are aligned via RLHF, using techniques detailed in the Gemini technical reports. The exact alignment recipe for Gemini 3.5 (2026) has not been published in a standalone paper.
- quote: No direct quote — Gemini 1.5 technical report (2024) describes RLHF-based alignment; Gemini 2.0 and later methods not published in standalone papers.
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05530 (Gemini 1.5 report, mentions RLHF)
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2024-03 (Gemini 1.5), Gemini 3.5 method unknown
- confidence: medium

### [8] The survey unification paper (Raheja & Pochhi, Jan 2026) maps the entire preference-learning landscape into three axes — Preference Model, Regularization Mechanism, Data Distribution — and identifies that RLHF (PPO), DPO, IPO, KTO, SimPO, and GRPO all share common failure modes (length hacking, mode collapse, likelihood displacement) that arise from specific combinations of design choices rather than any single method being universally superior.
- quote: "a proliferation of alternatives — Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), Identity Preference Optimization (IPO), Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO), Simple Preference Optimization (SimPO), and many others — has left practitioners without clear guidance on method selection."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06108
- source_type: primary (survey paper)
- published: 2026-01-03
- confidence: high

### [9] OpenAI's GPT-5.5 (scoring 55 on Artificial Analysis, the 3rd highest intelligence) and o3 reasoning model use RLHF with Process Reward Models (PRMs) for per-step supervision during chain-of-thought, as well as RLVR (Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards) for math/coding. The exact method mix for GPT-5.5 has not been publicly documented, but OpenAI's o1 system card (2024) confirms PRM + RL for reasoning.
- quote: No direct quote for GPT-5.5 alignment — o1 system card confirms PRM + RL for reasoning model training.
- url: https://openai.com/index/learning-to-reason-with-llms/ (o1 learning-to-reason blog)
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2024-09 (o1), GPT-5.5 method not publicly documented
- confidence: medium

### [10] AlpacaEval 2.0 LC win rate leaderboard is commonly cited as the benchmark for instruction-following alignment quality, but it is known to favor models fine-tuned on GPT-4 outputs (length-controlled win rate mitigates verbosity bias but not style-bias). As of July 2026, the top models include several fine-tuned variants that use DPO-based alignment.
- quote: "Length-controlled (LC) win rates alleviate length biases of GPT-4, but it may favor models finetuned on its outputs."
- url: https://tatsu-lab.github.io/alpaca_eval/
- source_type: primary (official leaderboard page)
- published: 2026-07
- confidence: high

### [11] OpenAI's GPT-5.5 has been observed at Artificial Analysis score 55 (3rd), but models like "gpt-oss-120b" (intelligence score 24) also appear — suggesting OpenAI runs evaluation experiments with open-weight model variants whose alignment method is unknown. The sharp performance gap between GPT-5.5 (55) and gpt-oss-120b (24) indicates alignment quality accounts for a significant portion of the score.
- quote: From the Artificial Analysis leaderboard: GPT-5.5 (xhigh) score 55, gpt-oss-120b (high) score 24 — both by OpenAI.
- url: https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-07
- confidence: medium
