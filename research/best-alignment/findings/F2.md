# F2: Survey Conclusions on Preference Alignment Method Comparisons

## Findings

### [1] No single alignment method universally dominates; method choice depends on data regime, compute budget, and task domain
- quote: "no single RL technique universally dominates, and optimal performance across diverse tasks typically emerges from thoughtful alignment of model architecture, RL technique, and specific operational objectives."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04136
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-07-05 (updated 2026-07-04)
- confidence: high

### [2] Data quality has a larger effect on alignment performance than algorithm choice — ~8% improvement from better data vs ~2.5% from switching between PPO and DPO
- quote: "Ivison et al. find that preference data quality matters more than algorithm choice—an 8% improvement from better data vs. 2.5% from PPO over DPO."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06108
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01-03
- confidence: high

### [3] SimPO is the recommended default offline method for most practitioners, offering the best trade-off of simplicity, stability, and performance on instruction-following benchmarks (31.5% AlpacaEval 2 LC vs DPO's 25.1% on Llama-3-8B)
- quote: "For most practitioners with moderate compute and reasonable preference data: SimPO offers the best trade-off of simplicity, stability, and performance."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06108
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01-03
- confidence: high

### [4] RLHF (PPO-based) remains the dominant paradigm for general alignment, with the highest stability for complex alignment tasks, but at 4-8x training cost vs SFT
- quote: "Our evaluation highlights key trends. RLHF remains dominant for alignment, and outcome-based RL such as Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) significantly improves stepwise reasoning."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04136
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-07-05 (updated 2026-07-04)
- confidence: high

### [5] PPO vs DPO is a nuanced trade-off: DPO wins with diverse offline preference data, while PPO wins when exploration beyond the data distribution is needed, formalized by the Coverage Separation Theorem
- quote: "The coverage theorem (Theorem 6.3) predicts this: DPO wins when data is diverse, PPO when exploration matters."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06108
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01-03
- confidence: high

### [6] GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) achieves the highest MMLU score (86.4% with DeepSeek-V2) among all RL alignment methods surveyed, outperforming PPO-based models on knowledge-intensive benchmarks, but underperforms on coding tasks (57.3% HumanEval with DeepSeek-Coder-V2)
- quote: "DeepSeek-V2, which employs GRPO, achieves the highest MMLU score of 86.4% among all models listed. This suggests that this RL technique may be particularly effective for enhancing knowledge-intensive capabilities. However, its GSM8K performance of 84.0% is lower than both Llama models."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04136
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-07-05 (updated 2026-07-04)
- confidence: high

### [7] Preference learning methods differ along three orthogonal axes that predict their behavior: (I) Preference Model, (II) Regularization Mechanism, and (III) Data Distribution — failure modes arise from specific, predictable combinations of these design choices
- quote: "This survey provides a theoretical unification of preference learning methods, revealing that the apparent diversity reduces to principled choices along three orthogonal axes: (I) Preference Model, (II) Regularization Mechanism, and (III) Data Distribution."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06108
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01-03
- confidence: high

### [8] Common failure modes (length hacking, reward overoptimization, likelihood displacement) are systematically explained: SimPO's length normalization directly addresses verbosity bias; DPO's gradient asymmetry (Proposition 7.3) causes likelihood displacement; reward overoptimization follows a predictable scaling law
- quote: "Our analysis reveals that failure modes—length hacking, mode collapse, likelihood displacement—arise from specific, predictable combinations of design choices."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06108
- source_type: primary
- published: 2026-01-03
- confidence: high

### [9] The UNA (Unified Alignment) framework, particularly its score-based MSE variant, achieves the strongest offline alignment results (average 30.92 on new Open LLM Leaderboard vs DPO's 28.53 and KTO's 28.56 with Mistral 7B), while online UNA is competitive with PPO-based RLHF at lower complexity
- quote: "UNA-score (MSE) method achieves the highest average score of 30.92, substantially outperforming the baseline Mistral model (28.61), as well as other approaches like DPO (28.53) and KTO (28.56)."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04136
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-07-05 (updated 2026-07-04)
- confidence: medium

### [10] For reasoning and knowledge-intensive tasks with large models (70B+), hybrid PPO+DPO approaches consistently achieve top-tier performance: Llama 3.1 70B (PPO+DPO) scores 89.1% GSM8K and 93.0% ARC-Challenge; Llama 3.1 405B (PPO) reaches 96.4% GSM8K and 95.3% ARC-Challenge
- quote: "For reasoning and knowledge-intensive tasks, PPO-based RLHF and hybrid methods combining PPO with DPO consistently achieve strong performance, particularly in larger models like Llama 3.1 and Qwen2-72B."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04136
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-07-05 (updated 2026-07-04)
- confidence: high

### [11] Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) significantly improves stepwise reasoning in domains with objective ground truth, with a single positive example boosting GPT-3.5's GSM8K accuracy from 56.8% to 72.5%; however it is limited to domains where correctness can be programmatically verified
- quote: "With just a single positive example, GPT-3.5's accuracy on GSM8K increased from 56.8% to 72.5%."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04136
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-07-05 (updated 2026-07-04)
- confidence: medium

## Suggested follow-ups
- How do self-rewarding / self-play alignment methods (SPIN, Self-Rewarding, Constitutional AI) compare quantitatively to DPO/PPO/SimPO — surveys from 2025-2026 do not cover these systematically
- What are the scaling laws for preference learning (analogous to Chinchilla scaling laws for pre-training) — identified as an open problem in Raheja & Pochhi (2026)
- How do PRM-based methods (process reward models) compare to outcome-based RL (RLVR) for mathematical reasoning — partial coverage in Srivastava & Aggarwal (2026) but no dedicated survey
