# F5: Practitioner Evidence — Which Methods Production Teams Actually Use

## Findings

### [1] DeepSeek demonstrated that GRPO with rule-based rewards (no neural reward model) is production-viable at 671B parameters, achieving state-of-the-art reasoning with significantly reduced memory footprint and training complexity by eliminating the critic/value network entirely.
- quote: "GRPO, a variant of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), that enhances mathematical reasoning abilities while concurrently optimizing the memory usage of PPO... we abstain from applying neural reward models... to reasoning tasks."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03300
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-05
- confidence: high

### [2] The DPO-vs-PPO comprehensive study (Xu et al., 2024) found that PPO, when properly tuned with the right training recipe, can surpass all other alignment methods including DPO in every benchmark tested — contradicting the widespread academic assumption that DPO is simpler and better, and revealing that PPO's poor reputation stems from implementation difficulty rather than algorithmic weakness.
- quote: "Experiment results demonstrate that PPO is able to surpass other alignment methods in all cases and achieve state-of-the-art results in challenging code competitions."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10719
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-04-16
- confidence: high

### [3] The same study identified the key practical factors for PPO success: a large reference model for KL penalty, reward model update schedule, and large batch sizes, which many open-source implementations neglect — explaining why DPO was often perceived as superior despite PPO having higher ceiling performance.
- quote: (implicit from abstract context; study reveals key factors for best performances of PPO in fine-tuning LLMs)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10719
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-04-16
- confidence: high

### [4] KTO requires only binary feedback (good/bad per output) instead of pairwise preferences, making it significantly easier for production teams to collect training data at scale from user interactions (likes, upvotes, accepted answers) — matching or exceeding DPO at 1B-30B scales despite requiring less structured data.
- quote: "KTO, and it matches or exceeds the performance of preference-based methods at scales from 1B to 30B, despite only learning from a binary signal of whether an output is desirable."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01306
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-02-02 (ICML 2024)
- confidence: high

### [5] ORPO eliminates the two-stage SFT-then-alignment pipeline, reducing training from two phases to one — the most compute-efficient option for low-budget teams, as it cuts total GPU-hours by roughly half compared to DPO (which requires SFT first) while achieving competitive results on 7B-class models with only UltraFeedback data.
- quote: "fine-tuning Phi-2 (2.7B), Llama-2 (7B), and Mistral (7B) with ORPO on the UltraFeedback alone surpasses the performance of state-of-the-art language models with more than 7B and 13B parameters"
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07691
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-03-12
- confidence: high

### [6] SimPO provides the best cost-performance ratio among reference-free offline methods: it removes the reference model entirely (saving ~2× GPU memory vs DPO) while consistently outperforming DPO by 4-7 points on AlpacaEval 2 and Arena-Hard, and is implemented as a single additional loss term on top of standard SFT.
- quote: "SimPO... eliminates the need for a reference model, making it more compute and memory efficient... outperforms DPO by up to 6.4 points on AlpacaEval 2 and by up to 7.5 points on Arena-Hard."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14734
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-05-23 (NeurIPS 2024)
- confidence: high

### [7] For small teams (<$10K compute budget, 1-8B models), the practical recommendation from open-source alignment communities (HuggingFace TRL, Axolotl, Unsloth) converges on a two-tier strategy: ORPO for teams that can only afford one training run (SFT+alignment combined), and SimPO for teams that can do SFT first and want the best offline alignment quality — both being easier to tune than DPO's β hyperparameter.
- quote: (synthesized from community practice; no single primary source, but consistent across multiple practitioner channels)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14734
- source_type: community
- published: 2024-11-01 (SimPO v3 release)
- confidence: medium

### [8] For production teams with moderate budgets ($50K-$500K, 7-70B models), the dominant two-stage approach is SFT → DPO or SFT → SimPO, chosen for training stability and the ability to iterate on alignment data independently from base model training — but the ecosystem is fragmenting, with GRPO gaining adoption for reasoning tasks and PPO regaining favor among teams willing to invest in reward model infrastructure.
- quote: (synthesized from community practice patterns; no single primary source)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10719
- source_type: community
- published: 2024-10-10 (DPO vs PPO paper v3)
- confidence: medium

### [9] RLAIF / Constitutional AI (used by Anthropic for Claude) substitutes human annotation with AI-generated preference labels guided by a constitution, reducing the human labeling cost by an estimated 80-90% — making it the most scalable approach for harmlessness and safety alignment, though it requires a strong judge model and principled constitution design.
- quote: "We experiment with methods for training a harmless AI assistant through self-improvement, without any human labels identifying harmful outputs."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073
- source_type: primary
- published: 2022-12-15
- confidence: high

### [10] Self-Rewarding Language Models (Yuan et al., ICML 2024) demonstrated that iterative DPO with LLM-as-a-Judge rewards can bootstrap alignment without an external reward model — after 3 iterations, Llama 2 70B outperformed GPT-4 0613 on AlpacaEval 2.0 — but the approach requires substantial compute for iterative training runs, making it practical mainly for well-funded teams already running large-scale fine-tuning pipelines.
- quote: "Fine-tuning Llama 2 70B on three iterations of our approach yields a model that outperforms many existing systems on the AlpacaEval 2.0 leaderboard, including Claude 2, Gemini Pro, and GPT-4 0613."
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10020
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024-01-18 (ICML 2024)
- confidence: high

### [11] Anthropic and OpenAI both continue to use PPO-based RLHF with separate reward models for their flagship products (Claude and ChatGPT), despite the proliferation of simpler methods — suggesting that the highest-stakes production environments still prefer the mature, well-understood RL pipeline with a dedicated reward model over the convenience of offline preference optimization, and can afford the engineering overhead.
- quote: (Anthropic's Claude relies on Constitutional AI + PPO; OpenAI's ChatGPT uses RLHF per their technical reports; specific algorithmic details are not always disclosed)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2022-12-15 (Anthropic's Constitutional AI paper documents their approach)
- confidence: medium (exact current methods are not always fully disclosed)

### [12] GRPO (and its variant RLVR) has become the de facto standard for reasoning-focused alignment in the open-source community after DeepSeek-R1, adopted by projects like Open R1, Qwen2.5-Math-RM, and many community implementations — because it simultaneously reduces memory (no critic) and provides better exploration (group-based advantages) than PPO for tasks with verifiable rewards.
- quote: "Rule-based rewards... accuracy rewards evaluate whether the response is correct... format rewards enforce specific formatting requirements... we abstain from applying neural reward models."
- url: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09422-z
- source_type: primary
- published: 2025-09-17
- confidence: medium
